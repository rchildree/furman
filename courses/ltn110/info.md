## Course description

Introduction to the fundamentals of classical Latin. Topics include pronunciation, basic vocabulary, grammar and syntax, and practice in reading basic Latin. Reading material is adapted from classical texts and cultivates an appreciation of Latin literature and culture.

## Our purpose and objectives

The goal of the course, quite simply, is to learn to read Latin. This is a worthwhile goal because it allows you access to the thought of the collective Roman mind — the history, philosophy, science, and art of the civilization that dominated the Mediterranean for centuries. Firsthand experience of the language prevents the distortions of translation, while also offering the benefits of thinking and expressing yourself in a second language.

At the same time, this is a bizarre activity: language is usually something learned slowly and gradually over a number of years. Our impatience and our desire to read Latin texts as quickly as possible means we must take shortcuts in the learning process. We will attempt to learn Latin in a systematic way by looking at the patterns that help us master large groups of words and wordforms at once, and by studying the structure of the language. Practically speaking, we will move from memorization of forms of words to more complicated ways of combining those words into sentences.

In LTN 110, we will take our first steps towards this goal. For our purposes, this means advancing in three main areas: *Vocabulary* (what the words mean); *Morphology* (how the words are formed); and *Syntax* (how the words come together to make sentences).

In more practical terms, by the end of the course, you should attain:
- A knowledge of and familiarity with the basic syntax and structure of the Latin language,
- A comfort with the workings of an inflected language,
- A growing vocabulary in Latin, and
- Increasing practice reading adapted and simplified Latin.
